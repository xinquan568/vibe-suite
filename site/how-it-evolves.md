# How it evolves

The rulebook is not fixed. What maintainers accept, reject and argue with becomes evidence, and that
evidence feeds back into the rules.

**Outcomes are recorded per rule.** Every pull request carries the fingerprints of the findings it
addresses, so merges and rejections attach to specific rules rather than to a general impression.
A rule that is frequently proposed and rarely merged is visible as such.

**Disagreement is captured, not discarded.** When a maintainer explains why a suggestion was wrong,
that explanation is classified and kept. A rule with a pattern of well-argued objections is a rule
with a problem, and the quarterly review exists to act on that.

**Exemplars come from real repositories.** Where an audit finds an artifact that demonstrates a rule
particularly well, it becomes a cited example in the rulebook — so the guidance points at working
code rather than at invented snippets.

**Nothing edits the rules automatically.** Refinement proposals arrive as pull requests with the
evidence quoted, and a human decides. The pipeline is allowed to notice a problem and argue for a
change; it is not allowed to make one.
